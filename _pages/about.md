I am an astrophysics PhD candidate at the **School of Astronomy and Space Science, Nanjing University**, supervised by **Prof. Zhiyuan Li**. My research focuses on the demographics, environments, and accretion states of X-ray binaries in nearby galaxies.

My long-term goal is to build a time-domain population framework for accreting compact objects beyond the Milky Way. This framework combines variability on short and long timescales, spectral behavior, local stellar environments, and rare accretion phenomena to understand how compact-binary populations form and evolve.

## Scientific questions

- How do stellar environments shape X-ray-binary demographics and the distribution of accretion states?
- Which accretion regimes do X-ray binaries beyond the Milky Way occupy, as revealed by their spectral and timing variability?
- How do the incidence and variability properties of different X-ray-binary populations depend on stellar age, metallicity, star-formation history, and dynamical environment?

## Research approach

Decades of Galactic X-ray astronomy have established a detailed view of Milky Way X-ray binaries and the physics of compact-object accretion. In nearby galaxies, early **Chandra** and **XMM–Newton** surveys established population-level X-ray luminosity functions, but luminosity alone cannot capture the diversity of accretion states or their evolution. Time-domain observations add this missing dimension by tracing periodic signals, flares, dips, aperiodic variability, and long-term changes in flux and spectrum.

I work primarily with archival observations from **Chandra**, **XMM–Newton**, **Swift**, and **NuSTAR**, combining timing analysis, X-ray spectroscopy, and multiwavelength imaging. Linking these measurements to resolved stellar populations and local environments enables population studies across a range of ages, metallicities, star-formation histories, and dynamical conditions.

## Current work

I have conducted a systematic search for periodic and aperiodic variability among X-ray sources in **M31, M81, and Centaurus A**. This work has produced an initial extragalactic time-domain sample spanning coherent periods, short-term flares and dips, aperiodic variability, and long-term behavior. I am extending this foundation toward a comparative population analysis that connects variability and accretion state with local stellar environments.

Two unusual systems discovered within the larger sample illustrate how nearby galaxies can expand the known parameter space of X-ray binaries: **M31 UCXB-1**, the first extragalactic ultra-compact X-ray binary and a candidate black hole–white dwarf system, and **M81F4**, a persistently luminous source exhibiting recurrent minute-scale, super-Eddington flares. The M81F4 manuscript is submitted.

Complementary projects examine X-ray emission from star clusters in M31 and M33, the relationship between ionized gas and resolved stellar populations, and early-science applications for the **China Space Station Telescope (CSST)**. Together, these studies provide the environmental context needed to interpret the time-domain properties of nearby-galaxy X-ray-source populations.
