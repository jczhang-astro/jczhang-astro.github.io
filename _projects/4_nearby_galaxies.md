My broader research interest is the relationship between compact-object populations and their host environments. Nearby galaxies make it possible to combine resolved X-ray-source studies with multiwavelength information about star clusters, ionized gas, stellar populations, and galactic structure.

During an academic visit to the **University of Washington**, I worked with **Prof. Benjamin F. Williams** on ionized gas and associated stellar populations in M31 and M33. I also study X-ray emission from star clusters in both galaxies and the high-resolution census of Hα-emitting sources in M33.

As part of this work, I contributed to a multiwavelength study of the nuclear environment in M31, combining observations and hydrodynamical simulations to revisit the nature of its ring-like gas structure {% cite li2025ring %}.

I also co-authored **“NOEMA Observations of the Center of M33: A Parsec-scale Molecular Filament and an Ordered Gas-Star-Formation Sequence,” accepted for publication in The Astronomical Journal (AJ)** {% cite li2026noema %}. The study resolves molecular gas in central M33 and finds an ordered distribution of molecular gas, ionized gas, and young stars consistent with compression followed by massive star formation. [Read the paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35278).

## CSST early science

I serve as a co-investigator on three China Space Station Telescope early-science proposals: joint MCI–IFS observations of **M31**, joint IFS–MCI observations of the **Virgo Cluster**, and joint MCI–IFS observations of **M33**. These programs connect resolved stellar populations and emission-line structures with the environments that shape compact-source populations.
