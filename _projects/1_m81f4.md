**M81F4** is a persistently luminous X-ray source in M81 that produces recurrent minute-scale flares reaching super-Eddington luminosities. We carried out a systematic investigation with multi-mission archival X-ray data to characterize its long-term behavior and repeated flaring events.

## Key observational results

- M81F4 remains in a persistently luminous accretion state while producing recurrent, minute-scale super-Eddington flares.
- The persistent and flare emission exhibit similarly hard X-ray spectra.
- The flares show neither spectral cooling nor an additional thermal component characteristic of classical thermonuclear X-ray bursts.
- The combined timing and spectral evidence strongly favors an accretion-driven origin over thermonuclear burning.

## Physical interpretation and significance

Our favored interpretation is an accretion-powered close binary containing a magnetized neutron star, fed through Roche-lobe overflow by an intermediate-mass donor. In this scenario, the recurrent flares arise from instabilities operating at the disk–magnetosphere boundary. M81F4 therefore offers a rare observational benchmark for models of unstable accretion onto magnetized neutron stars at extreme mass-accretion rates.

M81F4 first emerged from the broader timing survey of X-ray sources in M31, M81, and Centaurus A {% cite zhang2026variability %}, demonstrating how systematic time-domain surveys of nearby galaxies can uncover compact-object phenomena that are absent or underrepresented in Milky Way samples.
